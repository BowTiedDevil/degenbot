Tangents dominate swap curves — the lemma that powers the profit envelope

Both pools below are concave swap curves: output grows, but the marginal rate (the curve's slope, equal to price × (1 − fee)) only falls as you push more through. For a concave curve, the tangent line anchored at any point stands on or above the entire curve — so a handful of straight lines can certify the best profit a path could ever achieve. Each region's entry tangent is anchored where a trade would enter that region — at the crossing point (C k, F k) — with slope equal to the marginal price there × (1−fee). Earlier shelves: higher anchor, steeper slope. Every line dominates the whole staircase: concavity is global even though liquidity is not. The envelope gate's bound is the lower edge of this fan of lines.
