Deepening opportunities in the block pump

The degenbot-bot crate — and its BlockPump::run_with_stream driver in particular — has been the codebase's hot spot through the A-series (ADR-028 PumpDecision) and B-series (ADR-027 dispatch seam) epics. This review says yes to deep modules everywhere: the pure decision producer + thin I/O driver shape the ADRs already chose is right. What follows is where that shape is incomplete — capsules that still leak, decision logic that still lives in two places, and investigation scaffolding that has accreted onto the hot path.

Vocabulary: codebase-design (module · interface · depth · seam · locality) Domain: CONTEXT.md · block-pump PumpDecision seam (A) · dispatch seam (B) Defaults: ADR-027, ADR-028 accepted — not re-litigated

Where we looked, and why

Recent history is concentrated on the block pump. Working backward from HEAD, nearly every commit touches bot_core/block_pump.rs, pump_fsm.rs, event_dispatch.rs, block_clock.rs, or the solver-state verifier. That's the codebase's hot spot, so it pulls our attention first (deepening pays off where change is frequent). We did not trawl every crate: degenbot-solvers (pure solve layer), degenbot-executor, and the _ffi Pydantic barrier are documented as settled seams (ADR-015, ADR-019, ADR-025, CONTEXT dispositions) and show no unresolved friction from here.

1 · Complete the PumpFSM capsule

Strong

Completes ADR-028 — does not contradict it

Files
bot_core/pump_fsm.rs · bot_core/block_pump.rs
Problem — the "pure producer" seam is only half-realized

ADR-028 picked a specific deep module shape: "the FSM decides; the driver executes", with the FSM owning "every which-effect, when rule." That intent is real in on_tick/on_settle/drain_decision — but the capsule is perforated in three places:

  • State is public and the driver mutates it directly — ~18 sites set fsm.current_block, fsm.current_metadata, fsm.publish_pending, fsm.first_header, fsm.block_metadata, fsm.ws_delivered straight from run_with_stream. The decision rules are no longer "owned" anywhere — they're split between the FSM's methods and ad-hoc driver assignments.
  • backfill_range threads four &mut FSM fields out (current_block, clock, block_metadata, publish_pending) and mutates them from outside the capsule — bypassing every method that would keep those invariants coherent.
  • fsm.on_header is dead in production. It is called only from unit tests inside pump_fsm.rs. The real header decision — the is_first_header / gap-backfill / current_block-advance / notify_block policy — is re-implemented inline and untested in run_with_stream. One concept, two homes, one of them untested.
Solution

Make the FSM the one home for every decision and every transition:

  • Make the state fields private; expose small transition methods (on_header, on_log_forward, on_tombstone, on_reorg_enter/continue/close, on_drain_promote, …) that encapsulate the current direct mutations.
  • Collapse the driver's inline header branch onto the real fsm.on_header — delete the parallel copy; the header policy becomes tested through the FSM interface (the interface is the test surface).
  • Fold backfill_range into the FSM as a single method taking &mut PumpFSM, so a backfill's cursor/clock/publish mutations stay inside the capsule.
Benefits — locality, leverage, testability
  • Locality: "which effect, when" is verifiable in one file again, instead of being diffused across 18 driver assignments plus three method families.
  • Leverage: every transition is reached through the FSM's interface, so adding a new rule or a new watchdog never touches the driver's match arms.
  • Tests: the header policy — currently only the bypassed on_header is tested — gets its production path exercised. The deletion test applies cleanly: removing the inline header copy and the field-threading concentrates the complexity into the FSM instead of just moving it.

2 · Seal the investigation telemetry behind one deletable seam

Worth exploring

Also restores ADR-028's purity claim

Files
bot_core/block_pump.rs (36 DIAG/diag_/probe hits) · bot_core/pump_fsm.rs (diag_* fields) · bot_core/solver_state_verifier.rs (*_probe, divergence_scan, staged_clock_probe) · bot_core/mod.rs (drain_dbg_log_buf, verify_dbg_enabled, trace_ws_log_dispatch)
Problem — the freeze/desync investigation left scaffolding on the hot path

A long-running live-mainnet freeze/de-sync hunt scattered its instrument on the very code it was probing: inline DIAG counters and stall probes interleaved with the decision loop in run_with_stream; diag_header_count, diag_log_count, last_diag_ms sitting inside the FSM — which ADR-028 insists holds "NO provider, NO timer, NO Instant" (a last_diag_ms marker is a timer); and a family of gated *_probe functions in the solver-state verifier. Reading the decision loop means reading past a wall of conditional telemetry, and "the FSM is pure" is no longer literally true. Most of it is flagged "remove once the freeze root cause is confirmed" — but separate investigation rounds have accreted their own layers instead of removing the last.

Solution

Introduce one telemetry/probe seam — a single module owning every counter, interval, stats-emission, and gated probe in this area, exposing a tiny API (on_header, on_log, snapshot). The driver calls diag.on_header(...); the 150 lines of inline tracing collapse. Because it's one seam behind one interface, the deletion test passes the other way: when the investigation closes, the whole thing is removable by deleting one module, not surgically carving 36 interleaved sites out of the hot loop. verify_dbg_enabled/pool_state_head freeze-probe logic and the FSM's diag_* fields move here, restoring the FSM's purity claim.

Benefits — locality (and a stronger FSM)
  • Locality: the decision loop reads as a clean sequence of decisions; telemetry is a separate concern you opt into, not a tangle wrapped around the loop.
  • Purity restored: moving diag_* out of PumpFSM makes ADR-028's "no timer lives in the FSM" claim true again, and reopens the door for candidate 1's private-state cleanup.
  • Testability: the FSM unit tests stop depending on telemetry fields, and the probe family gets a home where on/off behavior is asserted once.

3 · One delivery path to the sink — not two

Worth exploring

Real friction, but verify the divergence is not deliberate before acting

Files
bot_core/block_pump.rs · bot_core/drain_sink.rs · bot_core/event_dispatch.rs
Problem — two delivery modalities that must stay behavior-identical

The driver reaches the DrainSink two ways. The live loop defers all async work through DispatchOwner::dispatch to the background drainer task (the ADR-027/B4GX7C decoupling). But the backfill path calls self.sink.finalize_block and self.sink.on_drain synchronously on the pump thread, and touches set_last_solved_block/has_dirty_paths/record_logs_this_block directly even in the live loop. The B-epic already paid dearly to prove inline-vs-decoupled equivalence ("re-proven correct twice"); this is the same dual-path burden resurfacing under a different name, so the WS poller can still park behind GIL-bound Python::attach/Möbius work during backfill — the exact freeze the decoupling was meant to prevent.

⚠️ Frame with care: the direct calls may be deliberately non-async state-setters and the backfill-on-pump-thread may be an accepted cold-path cost. Verify before refactoring — but the two-modality split is the kind of subtle divergence this codebase has been burned by.
Solution

Route every sink state transition and async delivery through the single dispatch owner so there is one delivery path, and audit the backfill synchronous solve/finalize to decide whether it should ride the drainer too. Where a direct self.sink. call is a pure non-async state-setter, promote it to a method on the dispatch owner so the driver has one surface to talk to.

Benefits
  • Locality: one seam owns the sink relationship; the driver stops knowing which calls are deferred and which are synchronous.
  • Leverage + correctness: removes the "must stay behavior-identical" re-proving burden; a future delivery-semantics change is made in one place.

Top recommendation

Start with candidate 1 — complete the PumpFSM capsule. It is the highest-leverage and the most decisively supported: it finishes a seam the project already chose (ADR-028 is accepted and actively being built out), it sits in the recently-changed hot spot, and it converts an untested production path (the inline header policy) into a tested one behind the FSM interface. Candidate 2 is a natural second move — pulling the diag_* fields out of the FSM is a prerequisite for making candidate 1's state genuinely private and pure. Candidate 3 is the speculative one; treat it as a confirmation exercise on the backfill drainer path rather than a first task.

Order:  1. Capsule the FSM → 2. Seal the telemetry → 3. Unify the delivery path
